# Search Intent Analysis Methodology

## 1. Objective

The objective of search intent analysis is to determine what users are most likely
trying to accomplish when searching a query, based primarily on evidence from the
current search engine results page (SERP) and the pages ranking on it.

Search intent must not be determined from the wording of the keyword alone.

The SERP represents evidence about what the search engine currently considers
relevant to the query. Ranking-page content provides additional evidence about the
type of information, product, service, comparison, or action that appears to satisfy
the query.

The analysis should identify:

- Primary search intent
- Secondary or mixed intent where present
- Dominant page/content type
- Dominant content format
- What the searcher appears to want
- Important recurring themes among ranking pages
- How consistent or mixed the SERP is
- Confidence in the conclusion

The goal is not merely to attach an intent label to a keyword.

The goal is to understand what kind of result appears to satisfy the query and what
a page targeting that query would likely need to accomplish.


---

## 2. Required Evidence

Search intent analysis should begin with live SERP evidence whenever available.

For each organic result, use available evidence such as:

- Ranking position
- SERP title
- SERP snippet
- Ranking URL
- Page title
- Meta description
- H1
- Heading structure
- Main page content
- Canonical URL

Not every ranking page must be successfully extracted for the analysis to proceed.

If a ranking page cannot be opened or extracted, its SERP title, URL, and snippet
may still provide useful evidence.

Never invent missing page information.


---

## 3. Evidence Priority

Use evidence in approximately the following order:

1. Overall composition of the organic SERP
2. Page type of ranking results
3. Titles and SERP snippets
4. H1 and heading structure
5. Main content of ranking pages
6. Supporting metadata such as meta descriptions and canonical URLs

Do not allow one unusual ranking result to outweigh the overall SERP pattern.

Likewise, do not classify intent based on a single phrase appearing on one ranking
page.

Look for repeated patterns across multiple results.


---

## 4. Primary Intent Categories

Use the following categories as broad classifications.

### Informational

The searcher primarily wants to learn, understand, solve a problem, or obtain
information.

Examples of result patterns:

- Guides
- Tutorials
- Explanatory articles
- Definitions
- Educational resources
- How-to content
- Research or reference material

Examples of possible queries:

- how does local SEO work
- what is schema markup
- how to waterproof a roof


### Commercial Investigation

The searcher is researching or comparing products, services, companies, tools, or
solutions before making a decision.

Examples of result patterns:

- "Best" lists
- Comparisons
- Reviews
- Alternatives
- Product roundups
- Software comparisons
- Buying guides
- Vendor evaluation content

Examples of possible queries:

- best local SEO tools
- semrush alternatives
- best waterproofing chemicals
- top SEO agencies in Pakistan


### Transactional

The searcher appears ready, or relatively close, to taking a commercial action.

Examples of result patterns:

- Product pages
- Product category pages
- Ecommerce listings
- Service landing pages
- Pricing/order pages
- Booking pages
- Pages focused on purchasing, requesting, subscribing, or obtaining something

Examples of possible queries:

- buy epoxy resin Pakistan
- waterproofing chemical price
- hire SEO agency Pakistan


### Navigational

The searcher primarily wants to reach a particular website, company, product,
platform, or known destination.

Examples:

- semrush login
- google search console
- facebook
- ressichem contact


### Local Intent Modifier

Local is not a standalone primary intent category.

It describes a geographic dimension of the searcher's need and may coexist with
Informational, Commercial Investigation, Transactional, or Navigational intent.

Examples:

- "dentist near me"
  - Primary Intent: Transactional
  - Local Modifier: Yes

- "best dentists in Karachi"
  - Primary Intent: Commercial Investigation
  - Local Modifier: Yes

- "SEO agencies in Lahore"
  - Primary Intent: Commercial Investigation
  - Local Modifier: Yes

- "how does local SEO work"
  - Primary Intent: Informational
  - Local Modifier: No

Identify local intent when the SERP and query indicate that geographic relevance
is an important part of satisfying the searcher's need.

Do not replace the underlying primary intent with "Local."

---

## 5. Primary vs Secondary Intent

Search intent is not always binary.

A SERP may contain multiple types of results.

Identify:

- Primary intent
- Secondary intent, when meaningful

The primary intent should represent the strongest recurring pattern across the
organic results.

Secondary intent should only be reported when a meaningful portion of the SERP
supports a different user need.

Do not create secondary intent merely because one unusual result differs from the
majority.


---

## 6. Analyze SERP Composition

Classify the page type of each usable organic result.

Possible page types include:

- Blog/article
- Guide
- Comparison/listicle
- Product page
- Product category page
- Service page
- Homepage
- Directory
- Marketplace
- Tool/calculator
- Review page
- Forum/community discussion
- Documentation/reference
- News page
- Location page
- Other

Use the actual structure and purpose of the page rather than relying only on its URL.

Then summarize the SERP composition.

Example:

10 organic results:

- 6 comparison/listicle articles
- 2 software product pages
- 1 review page
- 1 informational guide

This composition is stronger evidence than simply observing that the query contains
the word "best."


---

## 7. Determine Dominant Content Type

Identify what broad type of page the search engine appears to favor.

Examples:

- Articles
- Product pages
- Category pages
- Service pages
- Homepages
- Directories
- Tools
- Community discussions

Do not confuse page type with search intent.

For example:

A comparison article may satisfy commercial investigation intent.

A category page may satisfy transactional intent.

A service page may satisfy transactional + local intent.

A guide may satisfy informational intent.


---

## 8. Determine Dominant Content Format

Within the dominant page type, identify recurring formats.

Possible formats include:

- How-to
- Step-by-step guide
- Listicle
- "Best" roundup
- Comparison
- Review
- Alternatives
- Definition/explainer
- Buying guide
- Pricing
- Checklist
- Case study
- Template
- Tool/calculator
- Directory/listing
- Product/category listing

Only report a dominant format when the SERP provides enough evidence for one.

Do not force every SERP into a content format.


---

## 9. Analyze Ranking-Page Content

When ranking-page extraction is available, inspect the content for recurring
patterns.

Look for:

- Topics repeatedly discussed
- Questions repeatedly answered
- Products/services repeatedly mentioned
- Comparison criteria
- Pricing discussion
- Features
- Benefits
- Problems being solved
- Geographic relevance
- Definitions
- Steps/processes
- Reviews or recommendations
- Calls to action

Repeated themes across independent ranking pages are stronger evidence than themes
found on only one page.

The purpose is not to summarize every ranking page.

The purpose is to understand what information or functionality appears necessary to
satisfy the query.


---

## 10. Infer the Searcher's Goal

After analyzing the SERP, describe the likely searcher goal in plain language.

For example:

Query:
"best local seo tools"

Possible conclusion:

"The searcher wants to compare available local SEO tools, understand their
capabilities and pricing, and determine which option best fits their needs."

This explanation is more useful than simply saying:

"Commercial intent."

The intent label should summarize the classification.

The searcher-goal statement should explain the actual need behind it.


---

## 11. Mixed SERPs

Some SERPs genuinely contain multiple intents.

Do not force a clean classification when the evidence is mixed.

For example:

10 results:

- 4 product/category pages
- 3 informational guides
- 2 comparison articles
- 1 forum discussion

This should be reported as a mixed SERP.

Explain:

- Which intent appears strongest
- Which secondary intent is present
- How the result types are distributed
- Why confidence is lower than for a highly consistent SERP

A mixed SERP is itself useful SEO evidence.


---

## 12. Confidence

Assign confidence to the intent conclusion.

Use:

- High
- Medium
- Low

### High Confidence

Use when the majority of results clearly support the same intent and page/content
pattern.

Example:

8 of 10 results are "best X" comparison articles.


### Medium Confidence

Use when there is a dominant pattern but a meaningful minority of results supports
another intent.

Example:

6 comparison articles and 4 product/category pages.


### Low Confidence

Use when:

- SERP composition is highly mixed
- Too few results were collected
- Too many ranking pages failed extraction
- Available SERP evidence is weak or ambiguous
- No clear result pattern exists

Confidence should describe the strength of the evidence, not the certainty of the
model.


---

## 13. Handling Failed Page Extraction

Failure to extract a ranking page does not mean the ranking result should be
discarded.

Use available:

- SERP title
- URL
- snippet

to classify it when reasonable.

Clearly distinguish between:

- SERP evidence
- Extracted page evidence

Do not infer detailed page structure when the page itself was not successfully
extracted.

If enough SERP evidence remains, continue the analysis.

If failures materially weaken the analysis, lower confidence and state the
limitation.


---

## 14. Evidence Rules

Follow these rules:

1. Do not determine intent from keyword wording alone.

2. Use actual SERP composition as the primary evidence.

3. Prefer patterns repeated across multiple ranking results.

4. Do not allow one result to define the entire SERP.

5. Do not invent information for pages that could not be extracted.

6. Distinguish observations from interpretations.

7. Do not claim that a specific page format is required merely because one ranking
   result uses it.

8. Do not claim that the search engine "requires" something based only on
   correlation within the SERP.

9. Do not infer why a page ranks unless evidence directly supports that conclusion.

10. If the evidence is mixed, report mixed intent rather than forcing certainty.

11. If evidence is insufficient, say so.


---

## 15. Search Engine Limitation

Intent conclusions are based on the SERP that was actually collected.

Different search engines, countries, languages, devices, locations, and times may
produce different results.

Do not describe a Bing SERP as a Google SERP.

If the evidence was collected from Bing, explicitly treat it as Bing search
evidence.

Do not assume the same ten pages currently rank on Google.

The underlying user intent may be similar across search engines, but this should not
be presented as proven without corresponding evidence.


---

## 16. Stop Conditions

Stop collecting additional evidence when:

- A sufficient number of organic results has been analyzed
- A clear dominant intent has emerged
- Page/content patterns are sufficiently supported
- Additional ranking-page extraction is unlikely to materially change the conclusion

Do not continue browsing merely to accumulate more text.

If the available SERP is too incomplete to support a reliable conclusion, report
the limitation rather than repeatedly browsing without a clear reason.


---

## 17. Recommended Output

Use approximately the following structure.

### Query

[query]

### Search Engine

[engine used]

### Primary Intent

[Informational / Commercial Investigation / Transactional / Navigational]

### Secondary Intent

[Secondary intent or "None clearly identified"]

### Confidence

[High / Medium / Low]

### Intent Modifiers

[Local / None identified]

### What the Searcher Wants

A concise plain-language explanation of what the searcher appears to be trying to
accomplish.

### SERP Composition

Summarize the major page types found.

Example:

- 6 comparison/listicle articles
- 2 product pages
- 1 review
- 1 guide

### Dominant Page Type

[page type]

### Dominant Content Format

[format, if clearly identifiable]

### Common Themes

Summarize recurring topics, features, questions, comparison criteria, or other
patterns across ranking pages.

### Evidence

Briefly explain the strongest SERP/page evidence supporting the conclusion.

Do not provide a long summary of every ranking page unless specifically requested.

### Limitations

Mention relevant limitations such as:

- Failed page extractions
- Small result sample
- Mixed SERP
- Search-engine limitation
- Missing content

### Conclusion

Provide a concise overall interpretation of the query's search intent and what type
of page/content appears most aligned with the observed SERP.


---

## 18. Example

Query:

best local seo tools

Observed SERP:

- Multiple ranking pages use titles such as "Best Local SEO Tools"
- Ranking pages compare multiple tools
- Pages discuss features, use cases, pricing, advantages, and recommendations
- Several pages organize tools into ranked lists
- Ranking content helps readers evaluate alternatives rather than simply explaining
  what local SEO is

Analysis:

Primary Intent:
Commercial Investigation

Secondary Intent:
Informational

Confidence:
High

Searcher Goal:

The searcher wants to compare local SEO tools, understand what each tool does, and
decide which solution is most suitable for their needs.

Dominant Page Type:
Comparison/listicle article

Dominant Content Format:
Ranked "best tools" roundup

Common Themes:

- Tool features
- Use cases
- Pricing
- Recommendations
- Comparisons
- Local rank tracking
- Listing/review management

Conclusion:

The query is primarily commercial investigation. The observed SERP favors
comparison-style content that helps users evaluate several local SEO tools rather
than a purely educational article about local SEO or a single product page.
